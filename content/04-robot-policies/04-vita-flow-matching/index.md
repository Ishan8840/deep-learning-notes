**Vision-to-Action Flow Matching (VITA)** learns to transform observation latents directly into action latents. Its starting point comes from the observation rather than sampled Gaussian noise.

## Motivation

Conventional [Diffusion Policy](../02-diffusion-policy/index.md) and [Flow Matching](../../02-generative-models/05-flow-matching/index.md) policies repeatedly inject observations through conditioning modules such as cross-[attention](../../01-foundations/05-attention/index.md), AdaLN, or FiLM.

- **Cross-attention** computes interactions between query and observation tokens. Its attention matrix scales with the product of their sequence lengths; this is quadratic when both lengths grow together.
- **AdaLN and FiLM** avoid that attention matrix but add feature-modulation operations during generation.

VITA puts observation information into the **source latent**, removing the need to inject it again through separate conditioning modules at each flow step. “Conditioning-free” describes the flow network’s architecture; the policy still depends on the observation.

The paper reports **1.5–2× faster inference** than its conventional conditioned flow-matching baselines, with competitive or better task success rates. This is an experimental comparison, rather than a guaranteed speedup for every implementation.

## Inputs and notation

Observations $O$ include images $I\in\mathbb R^{H\times W\times C}$ and, optionally, proprioceptive states $S$.

An action chunk contains a sequence of future actions:

$$
A\in\mathbb R^{T_{\mathrm{pred}}\times D_{\mathrm{action}}}
$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| $T_{\mathrm{pred}}$ | Number of predicted action steps |
| $D_{\mathrm{action}}$ | Dimensions of one action |
| $E_v$ | Observation encoder |
| $E_a$, $D_a$ | Action encoder and decoder |
| $z_0=E_v(O)$ | Source observation latent |
| $z_1=E_a(A)$ | Target action latent during training |
| $\hat z_1$ | Action latent generated by solving the flow ODE |
| $D_{\mathrm{latent}}$ | Shared dimensionality of $z_0$ and $z_1$ |
| $t\in[0,1]$ | Continuous flow time |

## Architecture

VITA has three main components, trained together:

1. **Observation encoder:** Maps $O$ to the source latent $z_0$. The paper uses a ResNet-18 vision backbone.
2. **Action autoencoder:** Maps the ground-truth action chunk $A$ to the target latent $z_1$, and decodes action latents back into action chunks.
3. **Flow network:** Predicts the velocity $v_\theta(z_t,t)$ that transports observation latents toward action latents.

The inference path is:

$$
O\xrightarrow{E_v}z_0
\xrightarrow{\text{flow ODE}}\hat z_1
\xrightarrow{D_a}\hat A
$$

The action encoder supplies training targets; it is not needed to generate an action chunk at inference.

### Why learn an action latent space?

The source and target of this flow must have the same dimensionality. Raw actions are typically much lower-dimensional than visual representations.

Compressing visual features all the way down to the raw action dimensionality can discard useful information. Instead, the action [Autoencoder](../../02-generative-models/01-autoencoder/index.md) learns a higher-dimensional representation that matches the visual latent size and provides a structured target for flow matching.

## The training–inference gap

With ordinary autoencoder training, the decoder learns to reconstruct actions from **encoder-produced latents**:

$$
z_1=E_a(A)
$$

At inference, it must decode **ODE-generated latents** $\hat z_1$, which may differ from $z_1$. A decoder that reconstructs $z_1$ accurately can still produce poor actions from $\hat z_1$.

**Flow latent decoding (FLD)** addresses this mismatch by generating $\hat z_1$ through the ODE during training and supervising its decoded actions. Gradients pass through both the decoder and the ODE steps, tying the generated latent space to accurate actions and helping prevent latent collapse.

## Training objectives

### Flow matching loss

Interpolate between paired observation and action latents:

$$
z_t=(1-t)z_0+t z_1,
\qquad
\frac{dz_t}{dt}=z_1-z_0
$$

The network learns this target velocity using a squared-error objective:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
=\mathbb E_{t\sim\mathcal U[0,1],\,(O,A)}
\left[
\left\|v_\theta(z_t,t)-(z_1-z_0)\right\|_2^2
\right]
$$

### Autoencoder reconstruction loss

The action autoencoder uses an $L_1$ loss to preserve action information in the target latent:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{AE}}
=\left\|A-D_a\bigl(E_a(A)\bigr)\right\|_1
$$

### Flow latent decoding loss

FLD measures reconstruction error after decoding an ODE-generated latent:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FLD}}
=\left\|D_a(\hat z_1)-A\right\|
$$

Here, $\hat z_1$ is obtained with an Euler solver during training. Unlike the autoencoder loss, FLD supervises the complete observation-to-action generation path.

### Flow latent consistency: an alternative

**Flow latent consistency (FLC)** compares the generated and encoded action latents directly:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FLC}}
=\left\|\hat z_1-z_1\right\|
$$

FLD measures error in **action space**; FLC measures error in **latent space**. The paper relates them locally under assumptions about the decoder’s regularity and reconstruction error. They are not interchangeable without those assumptions. In the reported ablation, FLC also prevents collapse, but converges slightly more slowly than FLD.

The FLD and FLC equations above retain the paper’s generic norm notation; AE explicitly uses $L_1$ and FM uses squared $L_2$.

### Combined objective

The main objective presented in Section 4.4 combines FM, FLD, and AE:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{VITA}}
=\lambda_{\mathrm{FM}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{FLD}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FLD}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{AE}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{AE}}
$$

Reconstruction losses are shown per example and averaged over the training batch. FLC is discussed separately as a surrogate; it is not a fourth term in this displayed objective. See [Section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13231v4#S4.SS4) for the loss definitions.

## Inference

Starting from $z_0=E_v(O)$, solve:

$$
\frac{dz_t}{dt}=v_\theta(z_t,t)
$$

An Euler step updates the latent as:

$$
z_{t_{k+1}}
=z_{t_k}+\Delta t\,v_\theta(z_{t_k},t_k),
\qquad \Delta t=\frac{1}{K}
$$

Finally, decode $\hat A=D_a(\hat z_1)$. The paper’s standard implementation uses an MLP flow network and **six Euler steps**. Noise-free initialization therefore does not mean single-step inference. See [Appendix G.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13231v4#A7.SS1).
